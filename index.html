Employee Engagement at NTPC CMHQ

HR analytics · internship research

Descriptive study using two questionnaires (employees and organisers), analysed in Excel with percentage and frequency analysis. Survey data below is from 11 employee responses.

Key findings

  • Engagement lifts morale, teamwork and stress relief.
  • Recognition is seen as inconsistent.
  • Last-minute approvals hurt execution.
  • Fun activities (72.7%) were rated most engaging, then quizzes and cultural events (54.5% each).

Recommendations

  • Fixed engagement calendar
  • Recognition through the Samman Portal
  • Inclusive scheduling and clearer communication
  • Streamlined approvals and workload flexibility
  • Visible leadership participation

Method

Employees and organisers saw recognition differently

Organisers said

Participants are recognised through certificates, prizes and informal appreciation. Recognition for the organisers themselves is limited and informal.

Employees said

Only 27.3% feel always recognised. 36.4% feel recognised sometimes and 36.4% rarely.

Problems found and solutions proposed

Problem foundSolution I proposed
Uneven participationSchedule activities during or partly within working hours, rotate timings and target groups that join less.
Last-minute executionFix an annual or quarterly calendar, get approvals early and assign clear roles.
Inconsistent recognitionBuild a structured framework on the Samman Portal and recognise both participants and organisers.
Workload stress during eventsAllow flexible deadlines and supervisor support for participants.
Unclear communicationShare objectives and recognition criteria early by email, notice boards and portals, then post results.

My role: I designed the employee and organiser questionnaires in Google Forms, analysed the responses in Excel (percentages and frequencies), and wrote the findings and recommendations.

Small sample (11 employees, 2 organisers), so findings are indicative.

Patanjali: Tradition vs Quick-Commerce

Business strategy case study · team of 5

Quick-commerce discounts put pressure on Arogya Kendras and general trade, while younger buyers expect ingredient clarity and fast delivery. The proposal: give each channel a distinct role instead of making them compete. Select a channel.

Why this matters

75%+of e-commerce FMCG sales in the top 8 metros came through quick commerce (NielsenIQ, Oct–Dec 2025)
18%of FMCG sales in the top 8 metros were e-commerce (NielsenIQ)
400,000distributors represented in a complaint to the Competition Commission over quick-commerce discounts (Reuters, March 2025)
46%of quick-commerce shoppers cut purchases from small independent stores. This is the wider market, not Patanjali alone
12.5%of Patanjali Foods business came from modern trade, e-commerce, quick commerce and D2C together (Aug 2025)
79%of Gen Z in India use Google or YouTube every day (Google)

KPI framework

  • Channel profitability
  • Retention and repeat purchase
  • Channel migration rate: are digital sales new demand or shifted demand?

Price governance

Offer bundles on digital channels instead of cutting the price of a single product.

Three-year roadmap

0–6 monthsDiagnose

Benchmark margins, price gaps and migration.

6–18 monthsPilot

Test channel roles in selected cities.

18–36 monthsScale

Expand only what is profitable and incremental.

Four options I compared

Traditional channels first

Protects retailer confidence and reduces conflict, but the brand becomes less relevant to digital shoppers.

Digital growth first

Better urban reach, discovery and convenience, but heavier promotions and thinner margins.

My recommendationOmnichannel segmentation

Every channel gets a distinct role, so channels stop competing on the same product and price.

Youth-aware Ayurveda

Keeps the traditional positioning but changes how it is explained: ingredients, benefits and usage.

Keeping Arogya Kendras relevant

A store cannot beat 10-minute delivery, so it should win on what only a store can do. These are recommendations from the case, not current Patanjali initiatives.

Better product presentationIngredient disclosureQR codesSamplingLoyalty programmesTailored recommendationsDigital orders for out-of-stock itemsWellness consultation where permitted

How competitors handle it

Dabur

8.4 million retail outlets in FY2024-25, with quick commerce inside e-commerce up 74%.

Himalaya

Positions itself as modern Ayurveda backed by science.

Hindustan Unilever

A kirana-centric, distributor-inclusive model with 1.4 million+ retailers on its Shikhar platform, and nearly half its packs made for specific channels.

Qualitative comparison from the case study, not audited market-share rankings.

My role: I analysed pricing, channel performance and customer migration across the six channels, and developed the KPI framework and the 0–36 month implementation roadmap.

Power BI Sales Dashboard

Data analysis and reporting · 2026

An interactive dashboard with KPI cards, slicers and visuals analysing sales across five dimensions: outlet type, outlet size, location, product category and customer rating.

My role: I built the dashboard with KPI cards, slicers and visuals, then wrote up the sales patterns and recommendations.

Use the buttons to see each outlet-location filter in action. Click the image to enlarge.

What the dashboard shows

  • $1.2M total sales, $141 average sales, 8,523 items and a 3.9 average rating.
  • Tier 3 outlets lead with $472K (about 39% of sales), ahead of Tier 2 at $393K and Tier 1 at $336K.
  • Medium outlets bring the most sales ($508K, about 42%), followed by small ($445K) and high ($249K).
  • Supermarket Type 1 generates $787.5K, roughly two-thirds of all sales.

Key insights

  • Fruits and Vegetables and Snack Foods are the top categories at $0.18M each, followed by Household at $0.14M.
  • Low-fat items drive about 65% of sales ($776K against $425K for regular).
  • Average sales (about $140) and rating (4) barely change by outlet type, so sales differences come from volume, not price or quality.
  • Grocery stores show the highest item visibility (0.10 vs 0.06) yet contribute far less than Supermarket Type 1.
